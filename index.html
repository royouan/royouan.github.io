Abstract

We introduce D2E, a dataset for autonomous decision-making that explicitly incorporates driver states (e.g., attention, cognitive load, and emotional status) and human evaluations of decisions in diverse driving scenarios. By combining sensor data, driver monitoring, and human-labeled decision quality, D2E enables systematic analysis of how internal human states influence safe and reliable autonomous control.

Motivation & Overview

Conventional autonomous driving datasets focus primarily on external environment sensing such as LiDAR or cameras, while largely ignoring the driver’s internal states. However, real-world decision-making in driving is strongly influenced by attention, stress, fatigue, and emotional conditions.

D2E closes this gap by capturing:

  • Multi-modal sensor data from the vehicle and environment,
  • Physiological and behavioral measures of driver states,
  • Human evaluations of driving decisions under various conditions.

Dataset & Tasks

The D2E dataset consists of XXX hours of driving recordings collected from YYY drivers in ZZZ different scenarios. Each episode includes synchronized streams of:

  • Vehicle sensor data (speed, steering, acceleration, etc.),
  • Front- and rear-view cameras,
  • Driver monitoring signals (e.g., gaze, facial expressions, head pose),
  • Human evaluations about the quality and safety of decisions.

We define several benchmark tasks, such as:

  • Decision Quality Prediction given driver state and scene context,
  • Risk-aware Planning under different cognitive or emotional states,
  • Driver-aware Policy Learning integrating internal states with external observations.

Experiments & Results

We benchmark a range of baselines including supervised learning, RL-based policies, and driver-state-conditioned models. Our results show that explicitly modeling driver states significantly improves prediction of human-evaluated decision quality, especially in ambiguous or high-risk scenarios.

Please refer to the paper and the GitHub repository for full implementation details and numerical results.

Limitations & Future Work

While D2E covers diverse scenarios and driver states, it still has limitations:

  • The dataset is collected in specific geographic and cultural contexts, which may affect generalization.
  • Our driver state measurements, though multi-modal, are still an approximation of complex internal processes.
  • Evaluations rely on human annotators, which may introduce biases and disagreements.

Future work includes extending D2E to broader regions, integrating richer physiological sensing, and exploring robust learning methods under annotator disagreement.
